  1. Assessing the Impacts of Urbanization and Climate Variability on Cropland Dynamics and Food Security in Punjab, Pakistan (2000–2025): A Multi-Scale Remote Sensing and Machine Learning Approach
    Adnan Abbas Shah
    Environmental Monitoring and Assessment, 2026
    Abs DOI Bib HTML

    District-scale cropland and built-up change across Punjab from 2000 to 2025 using multi-resolution land cover data, ERA5-Land and GLDAS-2.2 reanalyses, and Google Earth Engine. Cropland declined 12.30% while built-up area expanded 180.31%. Groundwater storage declined significantly, and a Random Forest model explained 92.96% of cropland variance, pointing to urbanization, climate variability and water balance stress as the dominant drivers of food security risk.

  2. Analyzing Water Demand and Supply Under Socioeconomic and Climate Change Scenarios Using WEAP: A Case Study of Kabul River Basin, Pakistan
    Humaira Abid, Javed Iqbal, Adnan Abbas Shah, and 1 more author
    Water Conservation Science and Engineering, 2026
    Abs DOI Bib HTML PDF

    Current and future water supply-demand dynamics in the Lower Kabul River Basin modelled in WEAP under socioeconomic growth and RCP 4.5 and RCP 8.5 for 2023-2050. Domestic and agricultural demand rise and unmet demand grows, but efficient irrigation and improved management offer substantial mitigation potential aligned with SDG 6.

  3. Spatiotemporal Assessment of Desertification Sensitivity in Ningxia, China, Using the MEDALUS Framework and Random Forest Classification (2001–2022)
    Munaza Nawaz, Wilson Kalisa, Zakria Zaheen, and 4 more authors
    Journal of Geoscience and Earth Observation, 2026
    Abs DOI Bib HTML PDF

    MEDALUS framework combined with a Random Forest classifier to map desertification vulnerability in Ningxia from 2001 to 2022. Farmland expanded to 65.29% of the landscape, grassland shrank to 10.13%, and bare land followed a U-shaped trend reaching 9.17% in 2022. High-desertification areas persisted across 9,552 square kilometres, concentrated in northern and central Ningxia.

  4. Hydrometeorological Assessment of Water Budget Components in Rawalpindi Division, Pakistan: An ERA5 Reanalysis and Machine Learning Approach
    Owais Ali, Junaid Ahmad, Adnan Abbas Shah, and 1 more author
    Journal of Nautical Eye and Strategic Studies, 2026
    Abs DOI Bib

    Water budget dynamics of Rawalpindi Division assessed with ERA5 reanalysis, GIS, remote sensing, SCS-CN runoff estimation, Mann-Kendall trend analysis and linear regression. Across 131 months, precipitation, evapotranspiration, runoff, soil moisture and storage change show strong monsoon-driven variability, high evapotranspiration losses, and declining runoff and soil moisture.

  5. Compound Drought and Heatwave Events over Pakistan (1981–2025): Observed Trends, Coupling Mechanisms, and Future Projections under CMIP6 Scenarios
    Adnan Abbas Shah, Aon Abbas, Muhammad Khalid, and 1 more author
    2026
    Under review, Environmental Monitoring and Assessment (Springer)
    Abs PDF

    Compound drought-heatwave events across Pakistan characterized with ERA5-Land, CHIRPS, MODIS LST and vegetation indices, LandScan 2020, and a five-model CMIP6 ensemble under SSP2-4.5 and SSP5-8.5. National heatwave occurrence rose 23.6% (27.1 to 33.5 days per year; Sen’s slope +5.79 days per decade). CDHW intensity increased 64.1% per decade and drought-heatwave coupling explained 90.8% of compound-event formation. Currently 98.6 million people and 40,643 square kilometres of cropland are exposed to extreme heatwave conditions, with occurrence projected to reach 247.1 days per year by 2076-2100 under SSP5-8.5.

  6. Entropy-Based Characterization of Drought and Climate Change Amplification in the Jhelum River Basin, Pakistan (1981–2024)
    Adnan Abbas Shah
    2026
    Under review, Hydrological Sciences Journal
    Abs PDF

    Shannon-entropy rainfall variability integrated with SPI, SPEI and SRI across 209 grid cells to identify drought hotspots and an eastern rain-shadow belt in the Jhelum Basin. Roughly 52% of the basin is drought-vulnerable, and a significant SPEI-SPI gap trend evidences warming-driven drought amplification.

  1. Flood Susceptibility Mapping in District Jhang Using Remote Sensing and Advanced Machine Learning Techniques
    Adnan Abbas Shah and Muhammad Farooq Iqbal
    In 2024 IEEE 23rd International Conference on Frontiers of Information Technology (FIT), 2024
    Abs Bib HTML PDF

    Six machine learning models (RNN, ANN, RF, MLP, SVM, KNN) trained on Landsat, Sentinel-2, SRTM, CHIRPS and Dynamic World data to map flood susceptibility across District Jhang. KNN and RNN reached the highest accuracy (AUC = 0.99), and 27.2 to 39% of the study area falls in high to very high susceptibility classes. A Google Earth Engine web app was built for interactive visualization.
