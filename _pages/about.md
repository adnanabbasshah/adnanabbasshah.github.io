I work at the interface of remote sensing, hydroclimatology and machine learning. My research asks how
drought, heat and land cover change interact across South Asia, and what those interactions mean for
water and food security in Pakistan.

I hold an MSc in Remote Sensing and GIS from COMSATS University Islamabad and a BSc in Geography from
Government College University Faisalabad. My MSc work quantified agricultural land conversion under
rapid urbanization across Punjab using machine learning and multi-decadal satellite archives.

Current work covers entropy-based drought characterization in the Jhelum River Basin, compound
drought and heatwave events over Pakistan under CMIP6 scenarios, cropland dynamics and food security
in Punjab, and marine heatwaves in the North Indian Ocean. Most of it runs on Google Earth Engine,
Python and R, over Landsat, Sentinel, MODIS, ERA5-Land and CHIRPS.

Alongside research I consult on geospatial problems: flood and drought hazard mapping, environmental
impact assessment support, Web GIS dashboards, and spatial data pipelines. I have delivered over 30
projects for clients in eight countries.

I am currently looking for a PhD position in Earth observation, hydroclimatic extremes or GeoAI.
